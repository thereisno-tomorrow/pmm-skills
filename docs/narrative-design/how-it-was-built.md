# How Narrative Design was built

This skill comes from a five-stage narrative workflow I used on several companies. I ran it with AI agents drafting the stages and me reviewing them.

## The workflow behind it

The method draws on two practitioners. Andy Raskin's strategic narrative, set out in his essay ["The Greatest Sales Deck I've Ever Seen"](https://medium.com/the-mission/the-greatest-sales-deck-ive-ever-seen-4f4ef3391ba0), opens with a big change in the buyer's world rather than with the product. Marcus Andrews built Narrative Design on that work: investigate how the audience's world has changed, what that does to them, and what they could do about it, before the product enters the story. His [2020 article](https://marcusandrews.substack.com/p/why-narrative-design-will-replace) and [2022 interview](https://www.launchnotes.com/blog/narrative-design-with-pendos-director-of-product-marketing-marcus-andrews) set out that reasoning. My workflow organised that reasoning into five stages (Change, Impact, Adaptation, Difficulty, Solution) with a review after each.

## Calls that shaped it

**Positioning comes first.** A narrative inherits its audience, alternatives and limits from positioning. I chose to make reviewed positioning, or equivalent direction, the entry point, so the story can't quietly pick a different customer or promise more than the product does.

**Review one stage at a time, then deliver two versions.** I chose stage-by-stage review over a single draft, and a detailed argument plus a concise translation at the end. The detailed argument carries the reasoning for the team; the short version is what an audience actually hears.

**Ground the "why now" in evidence.** In one piece of research, two survey figures seemed to show a decline. A closer look showed they counted different populations, so each was described on its own terms. The skill's Change stage applies the same discipline: the "why now" rests on a change the evidence supports, and when that change is local, the skill tells a focused, situation-led story and treats it as a strong outcome.

**Describe the product at its true size.** In one narrative run, the Solution stage began to describe more than the product that positioning had selected. The rule that came out of it keeps the story credible: real customer difficulties stay in the story even where the product doesn't address them, and the product is described exactly as positioning defined it.

**Be exact about what is new.** The Change stage searches for an old, credible instance of any shift it proposes. That keeps the story precise: often what has moved is scale, cost or adoption rather than the idea itself.

## From workflow to skill

The skill adds what a reusable version needs: rules for entering partway through, for repairing one stage without redoing the rest, and for returning to research or positioning when a fact or premise fails. The [worked examples](../../skills/narrative-design/references/examples.md) use fictional companies, including one where the honest answer is a smaller story.

I chose the structure, the review contract and the two-version output, and decided which rules the skill keeps. AI agents drafted the files to that design.
