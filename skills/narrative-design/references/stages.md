# Stage procedures

Read only the active section. The entrypoint's human review contract applies to
each stage and assembly. Do not silently develop the full story behind a nominal
first-stage review.

## Change — why should this audience reconsider its current approach?

Start from the audience's world, not a feature launch. If selection is open, compare
credible shifts by evidence, audience relevance, temporal specificity, independence
from the product and explanatory consequence. Keep supporting context distinct from
the central change; relocate capabilities to Solution and proposed behaviors to
Adaptation. No fixed candidate count, manufactured winner or numerical scoring.

Explain before → shift → now: what changed, for whom, over what period and by what
mechanism where supported. A localized organizational change can support a localized
story; it does not establish a market-wide trend. Existing positioning's trend or
point of view is a candidate to examine, not a premise to force into this structure.

Challenge the strongest historical assertion with an old credible instance. Distinguish
invention from changed scale, adoption, economics, access or institutional use. An
old example disproves invention, but does not prove growth. Current availability
does not prove historical increase. Offline, use supplied history and expose the
verification limit; do not claim an exhaustive earliest-instance search.

**Review result:** selected bounded change, important candidate dispositions,
before/now evidence, historical challenge and unresolved questions. If no credible
change supports the intended narrative, recommend a narrower product explanation,
a genuinely different supported candidate or a stop. Do not label an evergreen
problem new just because the company needs a why-now story.

## Impact — what does the change cause or alter for these people?

Trace material direct consequences and downstream implications to the audience's
actual decisions, work, stakes and constraints. Distinguish observed effects from
causal hypotheses. Explain the links; do not list generic fear, complexity and risk.
Recognize opportunities as well as costs where warranted. Avoid inevitable winners,
losers, urgency and forecasts unsupported by evidence.

Make the practical situation recognizable through actual evidence or a clearly
labeled illustration. Separate user, champion and economic buyer when consequences
differ. Respect existing expertise: do not pretend experienced people lack skills
they already possess. Keep the protagonist and scope consistent with the entry brief.

**Review result:** consequence chain, source/status of important links, stakes,
audience boundaries and the decision Adaptation must address. A plausible scene
illustrates a mechanism; it does not establish prevalence or authentic language.

## Adaptation — what useful approach should they adopt?

Derive a strategic principle from the approved impact: what should change in how
the audience makes decisions or operates, and why would that help? Explain what
remains useful in the current approach. Avoid simplistic old-bad/new-good framing
and buying instructions disguised as a new game.

Develop the principle into a method someone could begin without this product:

- Relevant actions and responsible actors, sequenced or related as the work requires.
- Decisions and their criteria; outputs or evidence needed to make those decisions.
- A practical first implementation step using available/manual/alternative means.
- A feedback check, conditions to adjust or stop, and limits of applicability.

Choose only the principles and detail needed to make the method usable. A short
task may need a few concrete decisions; substantial narrative work may need a
small playbook. Do not impose a fixed number of steps, calibrated maturity score
or a broad educational-content program. If the audience already follows the
proposed behavior, identify the actual remaining change or preserve its method.

Test product independence: remove the product name and proprietary features. Can
the audience still understand the work and use another route? Can a reasonable
case end with no purchase or no intervention? If not, repair the method.

Name it only after the behavior is clear and only if naming helps. Prefer meaning
and comprehension over invented jargon. A visual is optional when it explains a
relationship. Naming searches and rendered assets are separate bounded work; an
empty search is not ownership or trademark clearance.

**Review result:** strategic principle, usable method, first action, decisions,
outputs, feedback/stop conditions, alternatives and limits. Keep proposed effects
hypothetical until suitable outcomes exist. Explain why this method addresses
Impact; a memorable name alone is insufficient.

## Difficulty — what prevents this specific adaptation?

Investigate the method's actual points of failure: information availability or
timing, coordination and authority, skill/capacity, incentives, workflow/tooling,
economic or integration constraints where relevant. These are lenses, not slots
that all need filling. Ask what has been tried, why it was insufficient and what
still works. Attribute supplied accounts and label hypotheses.

Group barriers by their real shared mechanism. Explain how each barrier interrupts
a decision/action/output in Adaptation and what assistance is needed. Separate
structural constraints from a claim that people are negligent or incapable. Keep
valid problems even when they fall outside the product's capabilities.

**Review result:** material barriers → affected method decisions → explanation and
evidence/status → help required. Explicitly leave unsupported barriers as questions;
do not reverse-engineer pain from the product feature list.

## Solution — how much of the required help can this offer provide?

Map barrier/help → actual capability/mechanism → audience consequence → evidence,
conditions and limits. Distinguish current product, roadmap, partner delivery,
customer configuration and human judgment. Do not treat a mechanism as measured
performance or customer value; do not erase genuine barriers the product cannot solve.

Walk through the minimum customer/product/partner sequence needed to apply the
adaptation. Show what remains the customer's responsibility, prerequisites and
tradeoffs versus a realistic alternative. A partial solution can be useful; an
unmet decisive barrier may disqualify it. Narrow the product role or reopen the
actual flawed premise rather than inventing excluded capabilities.

**Review result:** product role and walkthrough, barrier/capability/proof map,
unresolved help, fit boundaries and bounded next action. A proposed pilot should
allow the product or method to fail, including implementation costs and alternatives.

## Assembly and translation — how should the argument be communicated?

After the five stage decisions, assemble from their current approved versions and
explicit branches, never superseded drafts. Write the detailed argument as connected
reasoning a reader can understand without reading the ledger first. Retain source
handles and important qualifications alongside the claims they limit.

For a storyboard, give each narrative beat a clear main claim, supporting evidence
or illustration, speaker explanation where useful and the question/transition it
creates for the next beat. Five stages need not mean exactly five slides. For
messaging, choose a lead that serves the job; short product copy need not begin with
market change. Compress the argument without upgrading uncertainty or implying
outcomes, superiority or adoption that have not been established.

Test recognition of Change/Impact, credibility, usefulness of the method, realism
of barriers and sufficiency of product proof. Select the weakest consequential
premise and a concrete audience question/observation that could overturn it. Actual
feedback, when supplied, is evidence to interpret and propagate, not automatic
permission to pick whichever response makes the story more flattering.

**Review result:** detailed narrative, requested concise version, claim/use limits
and a small test agenda. Keep this artifact's status consistent with its owning
positioning plan when it serves an existing sales-narrative unit.
