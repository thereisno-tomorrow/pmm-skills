# Worked examples

All companies, evidence and circumstances here are fictional. These are teaching
illustrations of stage content; a live run still stops for review after each unit.

## A bounded change and a usable adaptation

**Inputs:** E1: East Plant changed from one to three shifts. E2: its four-week log
records two duplicate inspections and six unresolved jobs without incoming owners.
E3: ShiftLedger records unresolved jobs, owners and acknowledgment; it cannot
diagnose equipment. E4: paper handover books existed long before this software.

**Change:** East Plant now has more daily handoffs (E1). Handovers are not newly
possible (E4). No industry trend is established. Retain the local operational
change; reject the invention claim. The next question is what this changes for
supervisors.

**Impact:** The plant recorded duplicate inspections and unassigned work (E2).
More handoffs could increase coordination risk; the causal link is a hypothesis,
not proven by the counts. Check competing explanations such as maintenance-load
changes. The practical question is how incoming supervisors establish ownership.

**Adaptation:** Manage transfer of responsibility, rather than merely leaving
notes. A useful small operating method might be:

| Work | Responsible actor | Decision/output |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Identify unresolved jobs before shift end | Outgoing supervisor | Current jobs and known limits, including uncertain diagnosis |
| Assign the next responsible person and required check | Outgoing and incoming supervisors | Accepted ownership, or an explicit escalation if no competent owner is available |
| Acknowledge receipt and reconcile discrepancies | Incoming supervisor | Acknowledged record; disputed/missing items sent back for clarification |
| Review missed ownership and duplicate work | Maintenance lead | Keep, revise or stop the trial based on actual burden and results |

Start with one shift boundary and a paper log. Observe acknowledgment completeness,
unassigned work, duplicate inspections and logging effort without promising an
effect size. If records already work and failures arise from unavailable specialists,
more logging is insufficient; change the intervention or do not buy a log tool.
The product is unnecessary for understanding or beginning this method.

**Difficulty:** E2 supports missing owners in the sampled record; it does not yet
explain why. Investigate whether responsibility was unassigned, records fragmented,
or staff unable to accept the work. Those explanations require different help.
Do not invent a lazy supervisor or a customer quotation.

**Solution:** E3 supports shared ownership and acknowledgment records. Supervisors
still assign responsibility; technicians diagnose equipment. A shared log may help
fragmented records, but it does not supply missing expertise. A pilot could favor
paper logs or a staffing change instead. Product fit remains conditional.

**Faithful short message:** “Keep unresolved maintenance work and its next owner
visible across shifts. ShiftLedger records jobs, ownership and acknowledgment.
Test it at one shift boundary against your current process.” This does not claim
to eliminate duplicate inspections or establish achieved savings.

## No defensible external change

If shift patterns, requirements and economics are stable and paper handovers have
worked for a decade, “handovers were impossible until now” fails. Searchability may
be a valid product difference, but it does not establish a new audience world.
Recommend a product/alternative-led explanation, a different supported change or
an evidence request. Do not keep a five-stage why-now story alive by inventing urgency.

## Competent audience and a narrow repair

If experienced supervisors already acknowledge every handover, teach the missing
cross-department reconciliation step only if that is the actual supported gap.
Do not require them to learn a practice they already execute.

If a reviewed argument is sound but one Solution sentence falsely promises
diagnosis, correct that sentence and affected copy. Preserve the approved method.
If the method itself depends on automatic diagnosis, reopen that premise and its
dependents instead. In either case, a real diagnostic-skills shortage remains real.
