# Reading, argument mapping and founder perspectives

Explain thinking, not merely labels. These reading prompts make an original research workflow repeatable; they do not prescribe a formal coding taxonomy.

## Category reading

Read relevant source text and exchanges, not just the index or highlights. Ask what the person claims, why, what evidence/experience supports it, and which qualifications change its meaning. Preserve source links in provisional notes. Low-engagement statements can change the analysis; repetition and reach describe the sample's attention, not truth or population belief.

Group around substantive questions. “Reward design” is a topic. “Can repeated fixes make a reward signal reliable, or does each fix merely move the exploit?” identifies a disagreement. Keep independent issues separate: open distribution and rigorous quality control can coexist. Rejecting a fix need not reject the diagnosis; agreement with a multi-claim parent may not identify which clause is endorsed. Quote-posting or praising a release does not necessarily endorse its claims. Preserve ambiguity.

For each important argument, explain:

- The question and its relevance to the brief.
- Evidenced positions, named proponents, strongest reasoning and sources.
- Shared premises, actual disagreement, conditional compatibility and internal differences.
- Evidence status: estimates, direct experience, vendor reports, independent support and unresolved claims.
- Material uncertainty/missing voices and useful distinguishing observations where appropriate.
- Conditional company relevance, if a company is in scope.

No fixed count, two-sided format or definitive winner is required. Preserve hedges, units and populations. A source can corroborate a premise without supporting someone else's statistic. Duplicated reports do not become independent corroboration. Value disagreements may require choosing priorities rather than another experiment. Return to targeted collection for consequential gaps within the research boundary.

## Founder reading

Build a brief per person from their own evidence, including long-form material. The map's questions are an entry point, not a cage. Distinguish directly stated positions, context-supported interpretation, adjacent material and positions not established. Keep reasoning and conditions beside conclusions.

Find the distinctive contribution: mechanism, practical experience, alternative explanation, limiting case or solution. Identify recurring evidence standards, trade-offs, language and examples across the record. These can inform interview questions and communication hypotheses; they do not establish fixed psychology or a persuasion recipe.

Examine development and unresolved incompatibilities across dates/conditions. Avoid global pro/anti labels or assuming a later statement silently retracts earlier ones. Public silence means not observed within the collected record; activity elsewhere does not establish intentional silence, private beliefs or valuable whitespace. Compare cofounders without inventing internal responsibilities. Company accounts, outside endorsements and a founder's own statements are different sources.

Adapt this brief structure to the task:

1. Coverage and a short account of distinctive thinking.
2. Positions on relevant questions, reasoning, context-linked evidence and dates.
3. Recurring mechanisms/evidence standards and distinctive contributions.
4. Evolution, qualifications, tensions and unresolved questions.
5. Implications for the research purpose and assumptions needing confirmation.

For multiple founders, a short comparison can show shared ground, different emphases, genuine disagreement and uneven evidence. It must not replace substantive briefs. Correct the category map when founder evidence exposes an omitted position or fused axes.
