# Method card: assumption sensitivity

Use with [shared rules](shared-rules.md). Lineage: CIA/Heuer-inspired Key Assumptions Check adaptations in [evidence E4](lineage.md), premise sensitivity in [argument card 3](lineage.md) and thresholds/coherent changes in [decision D5](lineage.md). This is an adaptation of the recorded research synthesis.

## Trigger and omission

Use when a consequential conclusion depends on an accepted premise, when the dependency is disputed, or when robustness is claimed. Determine whether the premise is actually pivotal before questioning its truth. Reuse existing source, logic or calculation findings rather than run the same check again. Omit speculation about an assumption whose variation cannot affect the scoped answer or whose role is already adequately settled.

## Inputs

Retain the target conclusion, its material supporting routes, the assumed premise and current support, plus defensible alternative conditions/ranges if available. For a decision, retain the objective, feasible options and constraints. Distinguish a factual/predictive premise from an owner's preference or a rule about eligibility.

## Operation

1. Identify the precise dependency: why would this premise be needed for that conclusion? Check whether the original argument actually uses it or whether it is a proposed reconstruction.
2. Record the premise's current support and source status. Supported premises may remain accepted. A dependency is not automatically a weakness.
3. Temporarily withhold the premise to inspect the offered support, or vary a condition to examine a possible world/forecast. State which operation is being performed. Neither operation is evidence that the premise actually failed.
4. Reassess affected conclusions. Does the route fail, does the conclusion merely weaken, or does an independent route sustain it? Keep independent support and distinguish unsupported from false.
5. Where interactions matter, examine coherent joint changes. Use only defensible supplied ranges or explicitly labeled conditions; do not invent probabilities, catastrophes or arbitrary best/base/worst scenarios. For simple quantified inputs, calculate an interpretable reversal threshold if useful.
6. Separate the conditional sensitivity result from the present assessment. Revise now only to the extent that actual evidence challenges the premise, a constraint/objective changed, or the original conclusion claimed robustness beyond its support. Otherwise retain the current conclusion with a useful reopening condition.

## Warranted result and verification

Retain the conclusion; pivotal or non-pivotal dependency; source/support; consequence under the variation; surviving independent support; and current judgment or revision trigger. Express this compactly rather than filling a mandatory table.

Check the dependency against the actual argument and supplied facts. Check that the variation is coherent, units/periods are compatible, and independent support was not accidentally removed. Verify threshold arithmetic from the original values. If there is no defensible basis for a varied range, retain the symbolic condition or request a necessary input; do not claim universal robustness from a few scenarios.

## Consequence, correction and stopping

A hypothetical failure yields “if this changes, the answer changes,” not “this is probably false.” A changed owner preference can change an action without changing empirical findings. New evidence or constraints require updating affected results, not repeating unrelated analyses.

Stop when the material dependency and its consequence are settled or available evidence cannot resolve them. Additional research must have a possible decision-relevant result and a reason its cost/delay could be justified. Do not demand research merely because every conclusion rests on some assumptions.
