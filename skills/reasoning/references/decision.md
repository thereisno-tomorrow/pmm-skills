# Compare options and recommend an action

Use for segment prioritization and bounded PMM choices. Recover the decision, objective, period, feasible alternatives, constraints and unresolved tradeoff owner. Distinguish factual uncertainty from an unsettled preference. Give conditional advice where priorities differ; customer research cannot choose leadership's values.

## Compare consequences before ranking

Separate hard constraints from preferences. Check genuine necessary conditions before ranking attractive options; a profitable option requiring unavailable capacity may not be an option for this period. Do not invent a hire, partner or smaller scope to make it feasible. Where the choice set is open, check whether the current course, a smaller step or a staged option could materially improve the answer. Respect deliberately fixed comparisons and identify any omitted possibility separately.

Use a common horizon, unit and cost definition. Distinguish revenue, contribution and cash; ongoing margins and one-time costs; reachable business and total market size. Check whether costs are already included before subtracting them. Use supported ranges or a break-even calculation when they clarify what would change the choice. Do not manufacture forecasts or assume every uncertainty must be quantified.

Compare options in prose or a consequence table. Check whether one feasible option does at least as well on the relevant objectives, then inspect tradeoffs where neither does. Opportunity cost concerns what is forgone by choosing an option; attach it to the supplied alternative and time horizon. A larger total market or prestigious logo does not silently become the objective.

Avoid default weighted scores. Formal scoring requires defensible scales, meaningful tradeoffs and preference inputs; arbitrary weights and ordinal sums create apparent precision. Use a specialized method only when warranted, within available expertise.

## Action and information under uncertainty

Identify which assumption, forecast difference or owner priority could reverse the choice. Distinguish a robust choice across supplied scenarios from a result dependent on one fragile estimate. Do not add a generic forecast disclaimer where a specific condition suffices.

A smaller action may limit exposure while producing useful information, but specify feasibility, eligible customers, commitments, cost and what it could teach. Reversibility concerns how readily the prior position can actually be restored; withdrawing an offer need not undo contracts, expectations or trust effects. It is a factor in the decision, not an automatic veto on broad changes.

Before recommending research, ask whether different findings could change the action enough to justify collection cost and delay. Waiting is not itself learning. A useful offer can be chosen for commercial reasons without proving the cause of prior losses; name the inference it cannot support.

Finish with the recommendation and decisive tradeoff, a conditional choice if priorities are unresolved, or a precise obstacle if none is feasible. Keep reconsideration conditions attached. If the choice depends on a disputed cause, use the diagnosis bundle only as far as needed. A defensible recommendation is not evidence that implementation succeeded or that a segment is universally better.
