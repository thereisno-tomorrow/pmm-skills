# Shared rules

Apply these standards across case, diagnosis and decision work. The [evidence, update and final-claim procedure](evidence-update-claims.md) specifies the shared operations. The bundle guides and selected method cards supply the question-specific analysis.

## Preserve the question and proposition

Recover the actual question, intended use and work limits. For choices, establish the owner's objective and hard constraints without inventing priorities. Infer clear context; ask only for missing distinctions that could change the answer and block useful progress.

Preserve consequential subject, unit, meaning, negation, strength, quantity, conditions, comparator and period. Separate the author's claim, its stated support, an inferred missing bridge and a proposed repair. A repair cannot silently be credited to the original argument. Resolve source disagreements through definitions, populations, dates and versions where possible; otherwise retain the conflict and its affected consequences.

## Choose and interpret the operation

Select a check by a concrete vulnerability that could change the answer, its supported strength, a controlling condition or the action. Establish meanings and source status before inference; comparable definitions before arithmetic; hard constraints before preferences.

| Consequential issue | Read and apply |
| --- | --- |
| A conditional rule is used to establish a conclusion | [Conditional inference](conditional-inference.md) |
| Non-observation is used to infer non-occurrence | [Absence evidence](absence-evidence.md) |
| A premise carries a conclusion, or robustness is claimed | [Assumption sensitivity](assumption-sensitivity.md) |

The table is not exhaustive. Retain relevant measurement, source-dependence, causal and decision operations; reuse an established finding rather than repeating it under a different name.

For deductive claims, distinguish inference validity from premise truth. A counterexample must satisfy every relevant premise and deny the conclusion. Not finding one is not proof. For empirical claims, assess support at the claimed strength and scope. An imaginable alternative is not evidence against the favored account, and perfect certainty is not required for a bounded finding. For recommendations, assess feasible options, consequences and tradeoffs against the actual objective. An unresolved cause need not prevent a justified action.

Keep distinct: supported at a stated scope; contradicted; unresolved because of a material dependency; and irrelevant/unexamined. A failed argument does not establish its opposite. A hypothetical vulnerability does not establish actual failure.

## Combine findings and check their basis

Combine findings by what they bear on, not by counting cards or averaging verdicts. Source fidelity, logical validity, causal explanation and action justification answer different questions. Preserve independent support when one route fails. If findings genuinely conflict on the same proposition under the same assumptions, investigate or retain the conflict and its impact.

Check the defect a selected operation could have: source support and scope for a factual assertion; original values, units and cost definitions for arithmetic; all retained premises for a counterexample; claim identity for a repair. Return a discrepancy, bounded result or unchecked dependency. The source is an anchor for fidelity, not automatically truth about the event. Reliability may need separate appraisal. These operation checks feed the update record; the final-claim check additionally examines the answer actually written.

## Explain, update and stop

Lead with the strongest justified answer and controlling conditions, then give the concrete reasoning needed to assess it. Introduce unfamiliar named concepts naturally: explain the problem, identify the relevant principle/method/bias, give its plain meaning on first use and show why it matters here. Use an accurate category rather than calling every concept a principle. Define once per visible conversation; re-explain if requested or confusion appears. Use bullets for parallel examples and mark untested explanations as examples. Avoid jargon piles, automatic criticism and empty caveats.

Apply corrections through the update procedure, retaining independent findings. Source access blocks only the inference that requires it; do not request inaccessible material if it cannot change the judgment. Stop with a supported bounded answer, or identify the smallest material gap that cannot be resolved. Suggest a next check only when its possible results could change the answer enough to matter. Routine follow-ups need a proportionate claim check, not a renewed audit.
