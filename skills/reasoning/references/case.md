# Build or assess a PMM case

Use for a positioning recommendation, research conclusion or the justification of a PMM choice. An audit needs the argument or enough material to reconstruct it. If only a topic is supplied, obtain the missing case or explicitly construct a proposed one when requested.

## Reconstruct faithfully

Identify the actual conclusion, its scope and the stated reasons. Distinguish the author's statements, your interpretation and a proposed repair. The bridge from evidence to conclusion is sometimes called a warrant; identifying a possible bridge does not establish that the author believed it. Preserve ambiguity when multiple readings are plausible.

Inspect the support that matters. Are several reasons genuinely independent, or do they all depend on one observation? If a premise weakens, which conclusions actually fall with it? A failed supporting route need not defeat all remaining support.

## Test the claim at its actual strength

Match the evidence to what the conclusion asserts. In positioning, distinguish a real buyer problem, ability to address it, message understanding, preference, differentiation and buying behavior. Success at one stage cannot silently validate the rest.

For a superiority claim, inspect the relevant alternative, population, conditions and outcome. For customer testimony, inspect the experience it supports before generalizing to a segment. For ROI, distinguish time reduction, attribution to the product, usable capacity, modeled monetary value and actual cash savings. Reliable arithmetic cannot repair an unsupported conversion between them.

Choose material objections from the case rather than running a fallacy catalogue. An appeal to relevant expertise can be reasonable; a prestigious title alone cannot verify a buyer claim. A weak study is not automatically a named logical fallacy. Explain the failed connection and consequence before applying any label.

## Preserve or repair

State the strongest supported conclusion. If it is adequate for its stated purpose, explain why remaining limitations do not defeat it. If it overreaches, show the missing connection and a bounded repair; do not invent favorable premises to rescue it. Distinguish an argument's weakness from evidence that the conclusion is false.

If a gap matters, propose evidence that measures the relevant claim. For message preference, a comparison involving the relevant audience can address preference; it still does not itself establish purchasing behavior. Specify the needed observation without prescribing a universal sample or full research program.

Finish with what can be retained, changed or left open. Explaining what caused a commercial outcome requires the diagnosis bundle; choosing a different course may require the decision bundle. Do not expand a narrow argument review automatically.
