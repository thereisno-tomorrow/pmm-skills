# Diagnose a commercial outcome

Use for explaining lost opportunities or a comparable bounded commercial change. An outcome and relevant observations are prerequisites; a stakeholder's favored explanation is a hypothesis, not the outcome itself.

## Establish what changed

Identify the contrast: which outcome, which people/opportunities, versus what, over which period? Check counts versus rates, denominator, qualification, follow-up maturity, measurement and product/commercial changes. Compare meaningful pre-existing groups where justified. A blended rate can change because the population mix changes even when within-group rates do not. That is a descriptive explanation; it does not establish why the mix changed.

Treat closed-lost records, still-open deals, no-decisions and interview nonresponse according to what the question requires. Seller-entered CRM and buyer interviews may repeat the same account. A reported reason is evidence about the buyer's account of the event, not automatically the isolated cause.

## Compare explanations using distinguishing evidence

Generate plausible mechanisms with buyer/process context. Allow interacting causes; a documented necessary purchasing requirement can block a deal even if budget also blocks it. Do not force one root cause or dismiss direct process evidence merely because it is not randomized.

For each material explanation ask: what would we expect to observe if it were important, and would a rival explanation predict the same thing? Evidence predicted by every rival does little to distinguish them. Seek an informative comparison rather than automatically demanding a contradiction. Separate missing evidence from evidence against an explanation; silence depends on how records were collected.

An evidence-by-explanation table may help a complex case, but do not sum supporting quotes, ordinal inconsistency ratings or invented likelihoods into a winner. A short initial list is illustrative, untested and nonexhaustive, not an Analysis of Competing Hypotheses study. Structured techniques do not certify a diagnosis.

## Separate historical explanation from intervention

Temporal order alone does not isolate a cause. Inspect concurrent changes, selection into exposure, reverse causation and the comparison needed to estimate an effect. Counterfactual reasoning asks what would have happened without the proposed cause or action; explain why the missing comparison matters in this case.

For an experiment or small offer, inspect eligibility, assignment, comparison, start/follow-up, outcomes and implementation. Sales-selected opportunities may close at a different rate regardless of the offer. A before/after difference or many closes can describe results without estimating incremental closes. Hypothetical results remain hypothetical. Random assignment helps a particular inference only if execution, measurement and follow-up support it; proposing it is not conducting it.

Finish with the established outcome, supported mechanisms, unresolved explanations and the smallest useful distinguishing observation if worthwhile. Preserve a documented blocker within its scope. Do not infer that fixing it guarantees purchase. If action is requested, move to the decision bundle using the actual uncertainty rather than waiting for an unnecessarily perfect diagnosis.
