# Method card: Intervention comparison

Use when interpreting or proposing a comparison of action effects. Construct the comparison the decision needs and check what the available observations actually identify. This applies selected causal-design principles; it does not perform specialist statistical estimation.

## Define the action and its alternative

Identify the target customers, eligibility point, action, feasible alternative, decision outcome and follow-up period. State which components differ. If a discount group also receives more sales contact, the comparison concerns that combined offer unless the design separates the components. Do not attribute the whole difference to discounting alone.

Use the business objective already established. A higher response or win rate can coexist with lower net contribution. Keep adoption, buying and commercial value distinct, and do not silently change the owner's objective to match the easiest available measure.

## Reconstruct who entered each group and when

Identify the unit assigned and the unit measured: visitor, account, opportunity or rep are not interchangeable. Account members, co-owned opportunities and shared reps can cross exposure groups. Establish stable assignment before responses are known, how exposure is maintained, and how overrides, contamination or noncompliance are recorded. A nominal rep split does not ensure a clean comparison when both groups contact the same buyer.

For an assigned-policy question, retain all eligible assigned units in the main comparison, including nonresponders and nonbuyers. Conditioning on accepting the offer, attending a demo, signing up or activating selects a group that the action itself can change. A rate among those people can be useful descriptively while answering a different question. Reconstruct the original denominators rather than merely saying “selection bias.” Keep separately labeled subgroup descriptions when useful.

If assignment was discretionary, identify the specific pre-existing differences that could affect outcomes and whether supplied evidence separates them from the action. Do not convert an adjusted-looking table or a plausible mechanism into a causal estimate. A randomized design also needs stable exposure, adequate outcome capture and an appropriate unit; the word “randomized” alone does not settle these.

## Align outcomes and calculate the relevant contrast

Use a common time origin and sufficiently comparable follow-up. Distinguish incomplete outcomes from losses, and proxies from the commercial outcome. Check whether every assigned unit's outcome is captured. A short-window response report does not directly answer a longer purchase question.

Calculate supplied contrasts on the relevant denominator and show the consequential arithmetic. If costs are available, use net consequences over the stated period, accounting once for each relevant cost. Preserve the difference between a sample's observed advantage and its precision for the next population. Do not invent confidence intervals, sample sizes or probabilities, and do not interpret lack of significance as proof of no effect.

For example, a message can attract more trial users and more total activated accounts while lowering activation among trial users. Choosing between messages for an eligible audience requires outcomes per assigned account; choosing on profit also requires the relevant costs. Neither conditional activation alone nor total activations alone answers every commercial question.

## State what the comparison supports and repair only what matters

Separate a descriptive contrast, an effect supported by a credible comparison under stated conditions, and an effect that the evidence does not identify. Name the concrete missing contrast rather than adding generic caution. When proposing a study, fill in a feasible assignment unit, stable exposure, handling of overrides, complete main denominator, outcome and time horizon. Do not just recommend “an A/B test.” If clean assignment is infeasible, explain the remaining limitation and consider a useful alternative without claiming it removes confounding.

Randomization is not mandatory for every local conclusion. Controlled process replay can establish a reproducible failure mechanism within its tested scope. Nor must uncertainty prevent a bounded commercial choice: recommend the option justified by the owner's criterion and available evidence, with material unresolved dependencies beside it. Do not demand another study after adequate positive evidence or claim that a proposed study has already happened.

Carry the resulting comparison, quantitative consequence and unresolved dependencies into the update record and final-claim check. If the result raises an explanatory question, use discriminating diagnosis rather than treating an intervention's success as proof of every historical cause.
