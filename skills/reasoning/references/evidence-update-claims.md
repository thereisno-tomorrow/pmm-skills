# Evidence reconstruction, update record and final-claim check

Use these together across all three bundles. Their purpose is to prevent evidence from gaining strength as it is summarized, assumptions from becoming facts across turns, and final wording from adding unsupported claims. Apply them to the consequential basis of the requested answer, not every sentence or every available record. For a short argument the basis may fit in one sentence; a mixed packet or changing recommendation may need a small table.

## 1. Reconstruct the evidence behind the claim

**Start from the question, before accepting the packet's conclusion.** Identify the few claims the answer depends on. Unpack terms such as ready, validated or preferred only where their meaning changes the result. Preserve audience, unit, time and comparator. Record which source actually says what; do not treat a summary label as a completed analysis.

**Trace each consequential claim to its accessible basis.** Prefer the underlying observation when available and material. Record enough of the supplied source, section, row/account ID, quotation or turn to relocate it. Distinguish directly inspected observations, a person's report, an inference and an assumption. A source may report an event without independently establishing it. If only a summary is accessible, assess what follows from that summary and say when the underlying material was not verified. Do not reconstruct missing content as though it were inspected.

**Follow transformations and source families.** Identify consequential changes from observation to interview summary to slide to recommendation. Several artifacts repeating the same study are one evidence family for that finding. Separate any genuinely new observation or expert judgment they add; do not erase useful distinct contributions merely because sources overlap. Do not invent degrees of independence or count dependent endorsements as additional respondents.

**Expose the bridge.** State the link needed to move from observation to conclusion: for example, a measured response would have to establish the claimed preference, or a technical status would have to establish all launch prerequisites. Is that link supplied and supported, conditional, or missing? Distinguish an author-stated premise from an analyst's reconstruction. Resolve consequential differences in definitions, coverage, dates and versions before inferring contradiction. Retain unresolved conflicts locally rather than selecting the preferred story.

**Produce a usable basis.** For each pivotal finding retain: claim at its supported scope; source anchor and access status; necessary bridge; result or unresolved dependency. This can be brief prose. It should give the selected bundle/card actual inputs, not merely list concerns. Ask for an unavailable source only when resolving that specific dependency is necessary for the requested judgment; otherwise proceed with the supported answer.

## 2. Maintain the update record

Use a compact factual record when multiple sources, support routes or follow-ups make loss of context consequential. Keep it in the current conversation's working context; do not promise durable memory, write a private dossier or store it externally. The user need not fill out a form or receive the whole record.

Retain only:

- **Question and decision basis:** current question, owner-supplied objective and hard constraints.
- **Evidence and status:** consequential supplied facts/reports with anchors; assumptions and unknowns kept separate.
- **Findings and dependencies:** current bounded result, what it depends on, any independent support and what remains unresolved.
- **Relevant change:** what supersedes what, which dependent judgment changed and which finding survives.

On a follow-up:

1. Identify what actually changed: evidence, interpretation, assumption, constraint or objective. A challenge or expression of enthusiasm alone is not new evidence. A real owner change of priorities can legitimately change a recommendation.
2. Locate the affected premise and its dependent findings. If a correction supersedes a prior value, stop using that value; do not overwrite unrelated support. Distinguish a hypothetical variation from an actual correction.
3. Recompute or re-examine those dependencies using the relevant operation. Preserve conclusions supported by an unaffected route. If unresolved evidence now controls the answer, keep that dependency explicit rather than treating unknown as false.
4. State the practical delta: what changes, why, and what survives where that distinction matters. Retire the obsolete conclusion for current use while preserving enough context to explain the revision.

Do not let an earlier model-generated suggestion become a user-supplied fact. If a prerequisite must be satisfied before a future activity, retain it as a requirement until evidence establishes satisfaction; a booked activity alone does not establish every prerequisite. If history needed for a consequential update is unavailable, recover the smallest missing basis or state the limited basis rather than pretending to remember it.

## 3. Check the consequential claims in the finished answer

Inspect the proposed answer, including its headline, recommendation and reusable copy. Check claims introduced during writing as well as the original conclusion. A correct analysis can still end with an unsupported summary.

For each claim that could affect a decision, external promise or the user's understanding:

1. **Find its support.** Point to an inspected source or current supported finding. Derived claims need the actual inference or calculation, not a restatement of the draft. An old answer is not evidence merely because it appears in the conversation.
2. **Compare meaning.** Check who/what, quantity, time, conditions, comparator, strength and source status. Watch for some becoming all, scheduled becoming completed, required becoming satisfied, and proposed wording becoming tested wording. Keep necessary conditions attached where a reader will use the claim.
3. **Check the link.** Does that support actually establish the assertion? Recheck any new consequential inference with the relevant method. For a number, use the original values and units. Agreement with the draft is not independent verification.
4. **Resolve discrepancies before delivery.** Remove or repair an unsupported addition; narrow an overbroad claim; separate a proposed option from an observed result. Ask for evidence only if the unresolved claim is essential to the requested answer. Preserve a justified conclusion when deleting an unnecessary unsupported detail is sufficient.

Use this as a semantic check, not a keyword checklist or a promise of exhaustive verification. On a routine rewrite, preserve the established meaning without restarting the whole analysis or adding technique labels. Explain a material correction or limitation to the user; do not announce every internal check.

## How the three operations connect

Evidence reconstruction supplies the source-anchored inputs. The chosen bundle and method card establish what follows from them. The update record retains that result and its dependencies so a correction changes the right parts. The final-claim check compares the actual answer with this current basis. If it uncovers a new substantive inference, route that inference back through the appropriate operation; if it only finds an unsupported adjective or scope expansion, repair the wording and finish.

These procedures organize inspection and revision; they do not create missing evidence or guarantee that the model will execute every check correctly. They add no specialist estimation, corpus-processing tool mode or compulsory report format.
