# Method card: absence evidence

Use with [shared rules](shared-rules.md). Lineage: [evidence E5](lineage.md) and [diagnosis D3/D5](lineage.md), with the review correction that incomplete capture can retain evidential value. This is our procedural synthesis, not a quantitative detection model.

## Trigger and omission

Use when “not observed,” “not mentioned” or “no record” is being used to infer that an event or condition did not occur. The missing observation must bear on the requested claim. Do not demand access to additional records if a different adequate route answers the question and the absence claim need not be assessed. Do not use the card merely to reject every negative finding.

## Inputs

Identify the event, population and period; the expected trace; the inspected record; who or what captures it; and known coverage/reliability limits. Preserve the distinction between an underlying event, a record of it and a summary of that record. Missing capture information remains unknown.

## Operation

1. State exactly what was not found in which inspected material. Do not replace “not in the summary” with “never happened.”
2. Ask whether the event, if it happened, would ordinarily generate a trace of this kind. Check whether the collection process would capture it, and whether definitions, dropout, optional recording or selective summarization could hide it.
3. Check the relevant coverage: which people/events/periods were examined, what is missing, and whether coverage can address the claim's stated scope. Complete access to an unsuitable record does not establish absence.
4. Assess the significance of non-observation. Partial or imperfect capture can still count against a claim if the claimed event pattern would normally leave detectable traces. Known weaknesses reduce or bound that support; they do not automatically reduce it to zero. Do not manufacture detection rates or probabilities.
5. Reconcile conflicting reports by meanings, periods, populations and recording process where evidence permits. If the conflict persists, keep its implication unresolved rather than favoring the empty record by default.

## Warranted result and verification

Keep two assertions separate: what the inspected material reports, and what inference about the event is warranted. Possible results include only a report-level absence; qualified evidence against occurrence or claimed frequency; a strong bounded negative finding under adequate capture/coverage; or an unresolved question.

Return to the original record and actual capture information. Could the same observed silence occur under a plausible, evidenced capture weakness? If so, describe what that does to the inference rather than automatically declaring either absence or no information. If capture is unknown, do not invent reliability to strengthen the result. If the task is specifically source verification and the source is inaccessible, follow the shared focused-pause rule.

## Consequence, correction and stopping

Preserve a justified bounded negative finding. Where evidence only supports “not reported,” say that and retain the underlying-event uncertainty. A qualified absence inference must keep its basis and limitations when reused. New completeness/capture evidence can change the inference; simply repeating the same summary cannot.

Stop once the requested claim's support boundary is established. Recommend a missing excerpt or other observation only when it could change a material result. A complete negative finding is not a requirement to choose an action under uncertainty.
