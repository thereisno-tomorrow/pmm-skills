# Switching review

Examine reasons to stay, defer, try or switch. Identify what would actually change: a workflow, add-on, supplier or whole system. A trial and full adoption are different decisions.

Compare the strongest supported case for staying with the strongest supported case for changing. Weigh actual benefit against the current solution, smaller alternatives, transition effort, ongoing work, cost and risk. Separate product fit from budget, authority, timing and implementation readiness. If audience evidence or the current workflow is missing, identify the decisive gaps and limit the buying judgment rather than fill them with fiction.

Explain why each material limitation matters to this audience. Distinguish mandatory conditions, preferences, accepted compromises and unknowns. State the proof or change that would weaken each decisive objection. Recognize when existing evidence already meets a buyer's condition; do not move the goalposts, impose unnecessary features or demand another trial when the requested next step is justified. Keep local measurements local, without inventing universal reliability or financial return.

Return a bounded switching judgment, reasons to stay and consider change, unresolved tradeoffs, and the smallest useful next step. A walkthrough, reversible trial, continuation or justified deferral may each be appropriate. Say what result would change the decision. Suggested messages must preserve actual product limits; do not expand into positioning or another mode unless requested.
