# Landing page and sales pitch review

Use this mode to examine whether a message earns its requested next action. Establish the actual copy/script, audience, channel, sequence and commitment sought. Review the information realistically available: a page generally stands on its own; a live pitch can introduce proof later and allow questions. Do not assume hidden content, clicks or clarification opportunities.

Find the consequential break between promise, relevance, proof and action. Tie findings to exact claims or locations and the audience evidence. Preserve language and proof that work; do not manufacture objections to demonstrate skepticism.

Distinguish three causes before proposing a remedy:

- Communication: relevant value exists but is obscured, generic, misframed or badly sequenced. Clarify or relocate it.
- Evidence: a relevant claim outruns available proof. Narrow the claim or identify the evidence needed.
- Product fit: the offer cannot meet an evidenced requirement. Acknowledge the limitation or narrow the audience/claim; persuasive wording cannot supply a missing capability.

Judge proof against the next action's commitment. A short demonstration or dummy-data walkthrough can be justified before adoption-level checks. Conversely, a modest CTA does not excuse a false headline. Local demonstrations support only what they show. Avoid replacing message review with a purchase or switching recommendation.

Lead with whether the message earns consideration for that action, bounded by the evidence. Prioritize a few material findings: location, audience circumstance, effect, cause and smallest useful correction or test. Offer replacement lines selectively and only with supported capabilities and proof; do not automatically rewrite the whole artifact. Explain what to retain as well as what needs work.

If audience evidence is missing, limit conclusions to communication and clearly tentative audience questions. Close with a focused real-buyer check or justified next step, without predicting conversion.
