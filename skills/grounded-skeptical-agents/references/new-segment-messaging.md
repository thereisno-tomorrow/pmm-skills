# New-segment messaging

Use when the requested decision is which claims and assumptions in an existing message survive a specified audience change. Work from the original copy and segment context, target-audience evidence, actual product capabilities and relevant next action. Treat absent target evidence as a research gap; offer bounded hypotheses or questions instead of a grounded target judgment. Do not expand the task into segment selection or full positioning.

Identify the original message's consequential assumptions: whose problem it addresses, what they already know and do, the benefit they value, the alternative they compare against, and the proof or action expected. Compare those assumptions with target evidence rather than substituting a new industry label. Original-segment results establish what happened there; they do not establish the target segment's outcomes.

For each material claim, decide whether to retain it, change its emphasis or wording, suspend it pending evidence, or flag a product applicability gap. Explain the evidence and decision implication. Differentiate a comprehension problem, a different priority or standard of proof, and a missing capability. Better language cannot supply missing functionality. Distinguish readiness for a sample or conversation from readiness for production use.

Preserve supported common ground. If the target audience shares the relevant need and proof, recommend keeping the substantive message. Do not invent contrasts or impose sector-wide requirements unsupported by the specified workflow. Where target accounts differ, qualify the recommendation rather than create one composite buyer.

Lead with the transfer judgment and most consequential reasons. Show supported carryovers, necessary adaptations and unresolved applicability limits; provide the smallest useful adapted copy when justified. Keep caveats close to claims they constrain. End with focused real-buyer questions or tests that could change the recommendation, recognizing evidence already sufficient for the requested action. A defensible adaptation is a message hypothesis to test, not established segment fit, demand or adoption.
