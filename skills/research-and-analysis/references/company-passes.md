# Investigative procedures

Read the relevant pass and its [calibration example](examples.md) before execution. Choose operations that answer the company's real questions; an unsupported topic becomes a gap, not invented detail. Use the shared [completion gates](completion-and-handoff.md) after each pass. Compare technical approaches in product; named alternatives in competitor. Keep messaging/channel recommendations out of the first four passes.

## Product — explain the mechanism and its boundary

1. Build the **input → transformations → output → execution** chain from manuals, demos, release/support notes and specifications. For each stage identify where it runs, who operates it, prerequisites, dependencies and where responsibility passes to the customer, partner or third party. For a service, trace delivery, controls, capacity and failure recovery instead of imposing a technical architecture.
2. Decompose broad verbs such as “adaptive,” “autonomous” or “integrated.” Identify the separate mechanisms and timing they might mean. Map each to actual evidence, version/tier and operating conditions. Do not let evidence for one mechanism prove a different one.
3. Build a small specification/condition table around the questions that matter. Decode units and definitions; show any derived arithmetic. Compare like metrics and configurations. Resolution, linearity, accuracy and achieved full-system tolerance are different quantities. A component spec cannot settle end-to-end acceptance. Compare class-level educational claims against the actual shipped configuration without forcing a contradiction between unlike metrics.
4. Read releases/support records as evidence of limits: distinguish current exclusion, hard ceiling, fixed defect, mitigated failure and undocumented behavior. Date the change and keep tier/version scope. A fixed defect does not remain a current failure; a fix does not prove every configuration reliable.
5. Explain what conditions the mechanism requires, where it breaks and what remains the operator's job. Compare plausible technical approaches under the same application. Show tradeoffs only where evidence or an explicit conditional argument supports them. Product roadmap is not shipped capability.

**Result:** a mechanism/responsibility map; relevant operational specifications and normalized calculations; dated limits/failures; a defensible product verdict; prioritized gaps. Gate: a reader can explain how it works and its application boundary, not merely name features. See [product calibration](examples.md#product-mechanisms-and-specification-arithmetic).

## Buyer — reconstruct the purchase rather than invent a persona

1. Identify purchase units and separate user, champion, evaluator, economic buyer, approver/veto holder, procurement and delivery partner. A partner who specifies/deploys is not automatically an economic customer. Map formal vendor program design separately from actual deal evidence.
2. Express candidate fit as an intersection: **problem condition + necessary existing assets/capabilities + buying trigger + feasible deployment/economics**. Industry and company-size labels alone do not establish fit. Inspect relevant adoption/prerequisite base rates where they affect this intersection.
3. For a population claim, identify what must be measured: incidence within a group, number of eligible accounts, composition of adopters, stock or new flow. Inspect units, coverage, sampling, dates and classification limits. Compute separate quantities where useful; do not infer a trend across incompatible universes or pretend an aggregate category isolates the desired subsegment. See [buyer calibration](examples.md#buyer-populations-and-purchase-gates).
4. Reconstruct the operational job, economic justification and organizational approval task. Identify which budget line might be displaced, which gains are hypothetical avoidance and what costs/deployment burden remain. Source wage, utilization and full-delivery inputs before calculating payback. Vendor time-saving claims are not achieved customer economics.
5. Map **stage → actor → decision → proof required → failure/no-decision condition** from trigger through exploration, proof, justification, references, approval and deployment as appropriate. Seek buyer role descriptions, procurement procedures and authorized deal/interview records. Role descriptions show duties; adjacent studies support a transferred model, not this company's observed buying process. Do not assert a dominant failure point without actual deal incidence.

**Result:** buyer/purchase-unit map; condition-led fit hypotheses; bounded population quantities; buying/proof/deployment model; objections and non-adoption explanations. Channels and campaigns wait for marketing. Optional public discussions use [coverage controls](buyer-modes.md).

## Competitor — compare the decisions the buyer could make

1. Start from the buyer's job and actual decision, then enumerate direct peers, incumbents, adjacent approaches, bundled providers, in-house/manual/outsourced work and doing nothing. Several layers may compete for different decisions: complete outcome versus tool, architecture choice, extending an installed system, or relying on an existing supplier. These are useful questions, not mandatory four labels.
2. Search where those suppliers operate. If priority applications involve government buyers, check relevant procurement/award surfaces rather than relying on startup funding and commercial comparison pages. For other markets, choose the equivalent recording surface. Distinguish awardee, prime, subcontractor and product supplier where the evidence permits. An award record establishes its stated footprint, not a commercial sales census or actual head-to-head loss.
3. Compare the same application, current version and complete purchase scope. Record prerequisites, capability boundary, implementation/support responsibility, pricing/contract model and evidence. A software seat is not directly equivalent to a staffed full-service deployment; a subscription is not inherently easier to approve than capital equipment. Explain conditional risk/budget differences instead of assuming the winner.
4. For each relevant buyer condition, state where each alternative fits, fails or remains unknown, with evidence. Check hybrid capabilities and recent releases before claiming a clean conceptual divide. Do not treat public silence as missing capability, or public alternatives as those appearing in actual deals.

**Result:** buyer-condition × alternative table with scope, mechanism, commercial exposure and proof; credible substitutes; unresolved comparisons. See [comparison calibration](examples.md#alternatives-and-wrong-surface-discovery).

## Advantage — test the barrier a rival would have to overcome

1. State the proposed advantage precisely and the rival/substitute it is relative to. Separate today's capability difference, customer benefit and durable replication barrier. Ask: what must a funded competitor reproduce, who owns it, what slows replication and what erodes it?
2. Test the relevant candidates: IP, architecture, data/learning rights, switching dependencies, distribution, accumulated integrations, commissioning/process knowledge and support. For each record existence evidence, ownership/control, copy/substitute work, durability basis, counterevidence and remaining uncertainty. Do not force every candidate into present/absent when internal facts are missing.
3. Inspect IP at claim scope/status/owner and product fit, not patent counts or titles. Distinguish application, grant and unpublished-coverage uncertainty. Do not infer infringement or absence of proprietary work from public search.
4. For open substitutes, distinguish framework, application and deployable system. Inspect maintenance/runtime compatibility, implementation scope, setup/calibration instructions, support and real deployment evidence. Retain both sides: production is possible and deployment may still require work. One developer issue cannot establish universal cost or a quantified catch-up timeline. See [deployability calibration](examples.md#advantage-and-deployability).
5. Test design choices in both directions: native export may ease entry and lower an exit dependency; supported on-cell components may create another dependency; air-gapped customers cannot provide automatic telemetry, but optional air gaps do not exclude learning from other consenting customers. Data rights and actual learning remain separate questions.

**Result:** supported, rejected and unresolved barrier propositions with the reproduction mechanism and erosion conditions. A well-supported conclusion of no demonstrated durable advantage is valid. Do not inherit the run's early “moat” language without the later evidence boundary.

## Marketing — gather, verify/admit, then interpret

1. Read the four upstream analyses, their parked implications and the corrected buyer-stage map. Define what needs inspection: message/claim consistency, proof, discoverability, partner support, channels, team responsibilities and measurement as relevant to purchase.
2. Collect observations separately from analysis. A useful order is owned asset/technical surfaces, relevant comparator/partner surfaces, then accessible discussion/social material when needed. Log scope, dates, actual reads, exclusions and blockers. Historical tools, parallel agents and fixed sample sizes are not required.
3. Verify consequential positives and negatives with a method that can observe them. A server HTML fetch may miss consent-gated client tags; a sitemap/navigation check can distinguish missing assets, orphaned assets and broken migrated URLs. Indexation requires suitable evidence; search results alone do not certify coverage. “Last updated” and sitemap timestamps are not publication dates. See [marketing calibration](examples.md#marketing-admission-and-negative-claim-repair).
4. **Admission rule:** retain a finding in the marketing evidence/analysis when it affects a named purchase decision/stage or competitive position. State that connection. Leave irrelevant technical/SEO/social trivia out of the diagnosis. Put interpretations and parked readings in analysis, not the evidence register. Retain a material rejected finding/reason in the query/work log where needed.
5. For admitted findings separate existence, use, performance and commercial effect. A tag exists does not prove active campaigns, working attribution or revenue impact; partner listing does not prove productivity; public proof gaps do not establish missing private materials. Map available evidence to the buyer's required proof, then state the unresolved effect and discriminating check.

**Result:** purchase-stage/competitive support map, supported observations, testable bottleneck hypotheses and measurement gaps. Keep both insufficient qualified awareness and later-stage failures plausible until fitting evidence discriminates them. No campaign plan or predetermined bottleneck is required.
