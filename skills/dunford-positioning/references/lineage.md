# Method lineage and hard cases

This skill packages a methodology its author has applied repeatedly in positioning exercises across multiple companies. The research, strategic decisions, human reviews, rework and retrospectives from those applications shaped the workflow and its reasoning controls. The skill makes that accumulated practice reusable.

It supports internal and outside-in work, preserving a stage-by-stage human review structure. It is an independent implementation inspired by April Dunford's framework, not an official workbook.

## Method origins

- The most refined completed field exercise supplied the Stage 0–12 sequence, separate 11A–11D reviews, comparative system reasoning and activation/validation boundaries.
- Its mistakes register supplied controls against claims-audit overreach, hardened assumptions, fit/readiness confusion, unsupported customer proxies, scope circularity, availability-as-consideration and synthetic competitors.
- Its retrospective supplied dependency audits, removal/permission/rival/fresh-warrant tests and explicit treatment of remaining uncertainty.
- Repeated applications to other companies informed comparison, evidence handling and usability. The causal positioning sequence comes from April Dunford's framework; the wider workflow and review structure are this implementation's adaptation.

The underlying company records, transcripts and private audit paths are not distributed. Internal-mode guidance adds real customer and team evidence and ownership to the same process.

The installed method is self-contained and needs no access to those source records. Research on an actual subject still needs current evidence. Do not take dated example-company facts as live market facts.

## Hard cases

Situations where positioning work commonly goes wrong, and the behaviour the skill requires in each. Use them when reviewing changes to the skill. They describe consequential decisions, not required wording.

| Situation | Required behaviour |
|---|---|
| User starts an outside-in company exercise with only a homepage | Establish scope and useful missing product understanding; create/present Stage 0; stop for review rather than generating the entire chain |
| User approves Stage 0 but supplies no actual customers | Prepare Stage 1's bounded evidence or propose a skip/hypothesis route; do not claim public logos establish ecstatic customers or skip without a decision |
| Internal founder supplies a list of target industries and product claims | Attribute intent/product evidence; request actual customer/deal inputs where needed; do not treat the list as measured best fit |
| Every individual feature is matched somewhere, but the integrated offer differs from realistic complete alternatives | Investigate the combination and its comparative mechanism; do not reject it solely for component parity or invent a super-competitor |
| Competitor has a real integration that weakens the preferred differentiator | Include it in the complete comparison and revise confidence/conclusion; do not remove it to make the focal offer win |
| Scope selects one situation and the later value chain fits it | Preserve branch dependence and serious rivals; coherent downstream reasoning alone does not prove priority |
| A high-fit customer lacks budget or an immediately testable deployment | Keep strategic fit separate from technical applicability, buying opportunity, and proof readiness |
| A new fact invalidates an approved Stage 3 premise while Stage 7 is active | Reopen Stage 3, identify affected Stage 4–7 outputs, preserve valid evidence, and stop treating the old chain as approved |
| Stage 9 is approved and the user asks to continue | Run Stage 10 and stop for review; approval of assembly is not approval of a challenge that has not happened |
| User explicitly skips Stage 10 | Record the skip and consequence; any subsequent activation draft remains untested, with no claim of field validation |
| A second audience is absent | Propose omitting 11B explicitly; do not invent a delivery audience from a different company's exercise |
| An assessment tool has a preferred theme plus retained alternative themes | Assess each theme against its own customer consequence and complete fallback; the preferred theme's condition is not a universal eligibility gate |
| An approval arrives while a standalone summary still requests the old review | Record the decision and keep packet, index and summary status/next action consistent; mark retained requests locally historical |
