# Positioning stage guide

Read the active stage section before executing it, together with the approved inputs it depends on. The SKILL.md review contract applies to every unit below. These are workflow stage numbers, not Dunford book chapter numbers. Keep company-specific audiences, geography, products, and channel assumptions out until the current subject warrants them.

## Stage 0 — Scope and exercise contract

**Decide:** what is being positioned, for what purpose, using what evidence and participants.

Establish current product/company offer, product and geography boundaries where material, present versus planned capabilities, intended use, access mode, source locations, and decision authority. Determine the product and purchase object before selecting a customer winner. Existing labels, segments, competitor lists, and diagnosis are candidates, not approved positioning.

Internal: identify the accountable business owner and relevant sales, product, marketing, and customer-success participants. Agree who makes the real decision and which records/interviews are available. Do not assert that a workshop occurred merely because its preparation is complete.

Outside-in: state unavailable customer/team inputs and the provisional output boundary. Build enough neutral company/product understanding to scope the work; propose targeted research if it is missing. Do not infer a positioning problem solely from absent public proof.

**Packet:** exercise contract, evidence/access inventory, exclusions, working hypotheses, open branches, proposed artifacts, and concrete scope decisions. **Review:** approve scope and operating rules without pre-approving ICP, value, or category. A mandated commercial focus can be accepted as a strategic selection, with its evidence limits preserved.

## Stage 1 — Reference customers and customer circumstances

**Decide:** which customer evidence can anchor the exercise and what remains unknown.

Internal: seek customers who actively love the product, not an average across the entire base. Use actual win stories, adoption, repeat use, expansion, renewal, referral, and customer language as appropriate. Investigate why they chose it, what they would have used otherwise, the triggering situation, and who participated. Behaviour and reported reasons inform different claims. Inspect disconfirming cases without blending them into an enthusiastic cohort by default.

Outside-in: inventory identifiable deployments, customer-authored accounts, selected case studies, and public circumstances. Distinguish confirmed customer status, reported outcome, observable commitment, vendor selection, incentives, anonymity, and unavailable enthusiasm. A public logo or five-star rating alone does not establish the needed cohort. Rank cases only by the dimension evidence supports, not invented market importance.

**Packet:** accepted reference evidence, illustrative cases, exclusions, observed customer circumstances and alternatives, potential bias, and validation questions. **Review:** decide which inputs may anchor later work. If no defensible anchor exists, propose a skip or bounded hypothesis route with consequences; do not automatically skip or manufacture a proxy workshop. No happy-customer basis means the company cannot treat this as validated committed positioning merely because an outside analyst can still produce a thesis.

## Stage 2 — Vocabulary and positioning baggage

**Decide:** working definitions and which inherited frames must be re-earned.

Define positioning, alternatives, capabilities, value, best-fit customer, category, and trend in practical terms. Inventory current and historical labels, founding assumptions, investor stories, old versions, and website categories. Separate product description, category, capability, vision, and fashionable trend.

Internal: facilitate the team's actual alignment and explicit willingness to reconsider inherited framing; include differing information across functions. Outside-in: inspect public baggage and propose working definitions, without claiming internal agreement. The user decides whether that bounded work is useful or whether the unavailable workshop is explicitly skipped.

**Packet:** concise glossary, baggage register, assumptions suspended, and factual or legal terminology constraints. **Review:** agree vocabulary without selecting the eventual replacement category. Direct company terminology can constrain wording without establishing buyer perception.

## Stage 3 — Competitive alternatives

**Decide:** the conditional architecture of choices the customer would make without this offer.

Start from Stage 1's observed situations or explicitly selected hypotheses. Include complete internal-build, manual, incumbent, substitute, service, and no-change routes when relevant. Identify exact decision level, customer purpose, delivery owner, commercial boundary, realistic configuration, criteria, and geographic/product limits. Keep component suppliers and comparison-site categories from silently defining the market.

Separate observed shortlist alternatives from plausible scenario choices and rejected ghosts. Do not rank prevalence without suitable observations. For multiple audiences, keep distinct choices and decision roles visible without importing another company's audience split. If a candidate-generation source fails, find a substitute or expose the incomplete universe.

**Packet:** alternative map with provenance, conditions, evidence of actual consideration where available, rival situations, exclusions, and unresolved scope questions. **Review:** approve which conditional comparisons Stage 4 may use; approval does not turn public availability into observed consideration.

## Stage 4 — Strategic differentiation

**Decide:** the strongest defensible relative capability or coherent system hypotheses.

Compare complete alternatives fairly. Separate current capabilities from roadmap, customer-built additions, partner services, and vendor assertions. Test features, configurations, workflows, complete product-and-delivery packages, repeated workloads, and organisational capabilities. Do not require market-wide uniqueness or a win on every feature. Require exact coexistence only where the consequence needs it.

Investigate architecture, accumulated integrations, delivery knowledge, implementation burden, incentive differences, and copying paths. Keep unverified parity claims and silence from deciding the answer. Reconcile material conflicts with prior product/competitive analyses explicitly. A narrow claims audit must not veto potentially important hypotheses before value testing.

**Packet:** intelligible differentiation thesis and comparative capability/system map; source, ownership, level, evidence status, limits, durability, strongest counter-reading, and falsifier per material candidate. Include the verdict **shared capabilities**, **strategy fork**, or **shared core, fork unresolved** when multiple audiences/purchase objects make it relevant.

**Review:** accept facts, inferences, and worthwhile strategic hypotheses into Stage 5 with their conditions intact. Reject contradicted, immaterial, or unreasonable candidates; missing public proof alone is insufficient. Do not allow rejected candidates to return without reopening their premise.

## Stage 5 — Differentiated value and proof

**Decide:** what the capability system enables, for whom, why it matters, and whether it could drive selection.

Build explicit mechanism chains from each retained Stage 4 candidate. Identify the customer condition, enabled outcome, consequence, alternative, available proof, and missing measurement. Test complete value themes using the counterfactual purchase test; supporting features need not be independently purchase-worthy. Generic time/cost claims need a specific mechanism and relevant customer situation.

Distinguish purchase value, table stakes, qualification, objection handling, and proof. Map value to the champion/economic decision and risk answers to the relevant evaluators. Roles remain hypotheses unless evidence establishes them. Preserve audience-specific flips and organisational or repeated-workload value when warranted. Focus on a small intelligible set of themes rather than arbitrary feature buckets.

**Packet:** causal value maps, proof/status ledger, shortlisted themes, audience differences, competing explanations, and the formal outcome: **value evidenced**, **value hypothesised**, or **no value established**. A claim of genuine absence requires adequate internal evidence and examination of alternative explanations; outside-in failure to establish value is not proof it does not exist.

**Review:** choose which themes govern provisionally, which need testing, and which fail their mechanism. A strong strategic hypothesis can proceed without becoming achieved ROI or approved public copy. Surface a product/offer issue when value cannot be supported; positioning cannot manufacture an absent advantage.

## Stage 6 — Best-fit customers and champions

**Decide:** which observable customer conditions make the value matter most.

Derive fit from Stage 5 rather than a demographic chosen at scope. Keep four assessments separate: **strategic fit**, **technical applicability**, **opportunity readiness**, and **proof readiness**. Do not define the ICP as only funded, configuration-proven, deployment-ready deals.

Map candidate champions, economic buyers, users, specifiers, and gatekeepers. A job title, implementation role, or vendor target audience does not prove decision authority. Compare serious customer situations, including those unlike the current product's favoured form. Recommend a beachhead with its warrant or record explicit strategic selection and surviving rivals. When winning segments needs different capabilities or roadmaps, reopen Stage 4 rather than conceal the fork in messaging.

**Packet:** fit conditions and disqualifiers, role hypotheses, separate readiness checks, ranked segments only where warranted, provisional beachhead/branches, falsifiers, and strategy-fork outcome. **Review:** approve the customer model at its evidence level without converting product fit into measured incidence or preference.

## Stage 7 — Market category and winning style

**Decide:** the frame that makes approved value understandable to the best-fit customer.

Generate candidate frames from the reviewed chain, not just existing company language. Test each frame's likely inferences about alternatives, functionality, buyer, price expectations, and evaluation criteria. These are analytical predictions until buyer comprehension is tested. Compare fit to the complete system advantage as well as the entry use case.

Prefer an established useful frame when it works; category creation requires a reason existing frames mislead and a credible education path. Consider head-to-head competition, winning a focused submarket, or creating a new game. Assess resources and market context rather than treating category creation as a default. A platform claim needs specific shared-system value and cannot rest on convenience alone. A trend or future vision does not automatically constitute a category.

**Packet:** inference scorecard, recommended category/subcategory and winning style, rejected frames and trade-offs, and multi-audience compatibility/fork assessment. **Review:** approve the category before writing website hierarchy or treating a catchy label as the strategic answer.

## Stage 8 — Trend and point of view

**Decide:** whether an additional why-now layer helps this position.

Connect a real change in the customer's world to current strengths and the approved frame. Separate current delivery from future vision and consider market stability versus upheaval. Identify who should disagree or opt out. A relevant trend reinforces value; it cannot substitute for it. Omission is a legitimate outcome.

**Packet:** proposed trend/point of view or reason for omission, source/status, present-value mechanism, risks, and falsifier. **Review:** approve, revise, or omit the layer independently of the core category.

## Stage 9 — Assemble the positioning system

**Decide:** whether the full dependency chain supports a coherent usable position.

Reconcile current Stages 3–8; do not assemble from historical IDs or superseded approvals. Produce a detailed natural-language positioning document first, then a canvas and a one-page internal summary. The canvas connects alternatives → relative capabilities/systems → value → fit and proof; category and optional trend must support the same story. Keep coherent combinations intact rather than atomising them back into an isolated-feature grid.

Show audience-specific consequences when one product serves multiple roles. Do not require two segments, one fixed anchor, or one forced category when the evidence supports a genuine fork. Preserve branch conditions, role uncertainty, and customer-validation limits. Audit the story's dependency arrows as well as its individual sentences.

**Packet:** detailed document, dependency canvas, one-page summary, optional concise verbal formulation, and unresolved branch/claim boundaries. **Review:** approve the assembled provisional position only if a human can understand it without first reading the audit ledger. Return substantive errors to their originating stages.

## Stage 10 — Challenge and test

**Decide:** what survives serious objections, what must reopen, and what is ready for the intended use.

Red-team the complete system against strong realistic alternatives, status quo and no decision, role-specific objections, category inferences, durability, proof sufficiency, and surviving rival situations. Keep current delivery versus vision, one product versus strategy fork, and value versus risk answers distinct. A fluent narrative supplies no extra evidence.

Internal: use authorised actual sales/customer interactions and deal evidence when available; distinguish intended test, performed test, observation, and interpretation. Outside-in: run a sourced desk challenge and design field tests; never claim simulation establishes customer acceptance. Test major assumptions as early as useful rather than deferring every decisive question to Stage 12.

**Packet:** red-team findings, failure/objection matrix, falsifiers, revisions/reopenings, and field-test agenda or actual results. **Review:** obtain an explicit decision before Stage 11. If the user explicitly skips challenge, retain visible test debt and label activation work untested drafts. A past chain's challenge cannot approve a rebuilt chain automatically.

## Stage 11 — Operating-artifact plan

**Decide:** which artifacts the reviewed position needs and for which audiences/jobs.

Start from current Stage 9 and the Stage 10 decision. Plan 11A–11D as separate review units. Adapt their audiences to this subject; do not manufacture an integrator/partner track. Recommend explicit omissions where an artifact is unnecessary. Rendered decks, websites, publishing, or deployment require their own task scope.

**Packet:** selected artifacts, audiences, intended uses, approved input chain, evidence/public-use boundaries, and proposed omissions. **Review:** approve the artifact plan; this does not approve the as-yet-unwritten artifacts.

### Stage 11A — Primary sales narrative

Translate the position into a buyer conversation: market context, credible alternatives, desirable change, differentiated value, proof, objections, discovery questions, and next step. A live conversation is not a fixed marketing monologue. Trace narrative claims to the assembly; do not invent customer results to complete a story.

**Review packet:** narrative logic and claim trace, counter-reading, proof gaps, and permitted use. Stop for approval before the next selected unit.

### Stage 11B — Additional audience adaptation

Test whether another legitimate buyer, user, specifier, or delivery audience needs a different explanation of the same position. Preserve common product facts and distinct audience value/authority. Keep partner recruitment separate unless scoped. If the same position cannot serve the purchase object, reopen the relevant strategy decision rather than hiding the conflict.

**Review packet:** audience rationale, adaptation and source trace, shared versus changed inputs, and divergence verdict; or an explicit skip request if unnecessary. Stop for review.

### Stage 11C — Website message architecture

Define hierarchy, page jobs, category context, relevant segment/application pages, proof placement, and calls to action from the approved position. Approve architecture before final copy or site implementation. Intentional recommendation, descriptive product fact, and achieved outcome require different wording and proof.

**Review packet:** hierarchy/page roles, message and proof sources, gaps, public-use boundary, and requested architecture decision. Stop for review.

### Stage 11D — Qualification, proof, and content requirements

Translate fit and proof gaps into useful qualification/disqualification questions, proof-of-concept criteria, economic-case requirements, reference evidence, content priorities tied to buying decisions, and attribution fields where relevant. Keep fit separate from opportunity/proof readiness. Do not build a publishing factory merely to fill a stage.

Each assessment field or gate must preserve the scope of its originating value theme and customer circumstance: identify what is being assessed, and do not make the preferred theme's condition a universal fit requirement for other retained branches unless a reviewed decision explicitly excludes them.

**Review packet:** proposed operational tools and priorities, dependency trace, missing inputs, and measurement limits. Stop for review.

## Stage 12 — Validation, ownership, and maintenance

**Decide:** who must test, own, adopt, and revise the position, and what remains provisional.

Consolidate unresolved customer anchors, win/loss explanations, role authority, configuration/economic proof, and live-pitch tests. Identify accountable company roles and real decision rights. Internal: record actual owners and available measures. Outside-in: propose roles, questions, and validation agenda without claiming company adoption or permission to contact anyone.

Choose adoption/outcome measures appropriate to the actual motion, with baselines and observation limits. Propose a practical review cadence and triggers for product, competition, acquisition, market shifts, or recurring losses/objections. Define how a changed premise propagates into the assembly and selected activation artifacts.

**Packet:** validation plan/results, ownership map, measures, review triggers, propagation responsibilities, challenge debt, and remaining provisional claims. **Review:** approve the package at its actual maturity. Distinguish a completed outside-in exercise from a company-validated, adopted, or commercially successful position. Any excluded optional deliverables remain explicit decisions rather than implied completions.
