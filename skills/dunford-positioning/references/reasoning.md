# Reasoning and evidence

These controls distil a corrected field process, its mistakes register, and retrospective. They govern reasoning within the stages, not an extra series of gates.

## Evidence and access

Keep a claim's source, logical status, and confidence distinct.

| Source/input | What it can establish | What it cannot establish by itself |
|---|---|---|
| Product, code, documentation, or observed behaviour | A bounded capability, implementation, or limitation | Customer importance, achieved ROI, or competitive preference |
| Founder/team statement | Attributed intent, terminology, account of operations | Observed customer preference, buying authority, or market prevalence |
| Customer interview or account | That person's reported experience and reasons, at the sampled time | A representative pattern, objective causality, or population share |
| Deal, retention, usage, or win/loss records | Recorded behaviour within the measured population | A causal reason without an appropriate investigation |
| Independent comparison or measurement | Its actual configuration, sample, metric, and date | Universal superiority or performance outside the tested conditions |
| Vendor case study or logo | A claimed relationship or selected account | An independent enthusiastic-customer set or repeatable achieved value |

Label consequential conclusions as **established fact**, **strong inference**, **strategic hypothesis**, **rejected possibility**, or **unknown**. Keep vendor assertions attributed rather than laundering them into established performance. Separate confidentiality/public-use permission from evidence strength. Internal material can be strong evidence and still unsuitable for publication.

Customer validation depends on who supplied what and how it was tested, not on internal/external mode labels. Use multiple genuinely independent observations for a pattern; copied reports of one origin remain one source. Public-source scarcity does not establish low demand or lack of customers. Avoid universal numerical thresholds for enthusiasm or evidence sufficiency.

## Discover strategy before applying public wording

Investigate plausible advantages before deciding how they can safely be stated. A claim-safe feature inventory is not the entire differentiation method. Test features, coherent configurations, workflows, product-and-delivery systems, repeated workloads, organisational capabilities, and accumulated integration or deployment knowledge when relevant.

Composition of supported facts can support a useful inference. Missing proof may leave a strategic hypothesis worth testing. Neither composition nor a hypothesis label proves achieved value. Require exact coexistence/configuration proof when the proposed consequence depends on those components operating together. Do not demand every component be independently unique or independently purchase-worthy.

Assess copying through architecture, accumulated work, skills, incentives, and implementation burden. A describable feature is not automatically easy to copy. A rival's parity claim is a claim to inspect, not a command to surrender a candidate advantage. Rival silence likewise does not prove absence.

## Start with the customer decision

Define the purpose, situation, and decision level before letting the best-documented product determine the comparison universe. Keep complete routes, technical components, delivery owners, commercial models, and terminal outcomes distinct. They can interact but are not interchangeable alternatives in a tidy flat list.

For each serious comparator, ask:

- Could this customer literally choose this complete route in this situation?
- Have we combined the best parts of several rivals into an imaginary super-competitor?
- Have we removed a real component, service, integration, or responsibility to make a rival weaker?
- Are we comparing at the level where the proposed advantage actually occurs?

Keep observed shortlist/deal alternatives separate from plausible scenario alternatives and rejected ghosts. Public availability supports possibility, not frequency or rank. A registry or comparison page can generate candidates; only suitable customer/deal evidence establishes actual consideration.

Distinguish retaining the status quo, deferring a project, rescoping it, abandoning a decision after evaluation, and declining to serve a project where the distinctions affect the buying choice. Do not claim all occur in every market.

## Scope is not customer evidence

Scope authorises examination. It does not prove a situation is prevalent, urgent, preferred, or commercially primary. Keep product facts, customer-situation hypotheses, and strategic selections separately identifiable.

The user may select a plausible branch for strategic usefulness even when incidence cannot be ranked. Record the choice as judgment, why it is worth pursuing, serious rivals, and reopening evidence. Do not describe a chosen branch as evidence-based consolidation. Preserve several branches when no defensible selection or consolidation exists.

Do not derive an installed-base ICP merely because scope assumed an installed-base product and the resulting value model fits that scope. Downstream coherence tests a branch; it does not independently validate the branch's selection.

## Make each dependency earn its consequence

For a material transition, explain the new warrant: new evidence or valid reasoning that supports the new claim type. Merely repeating a premise, obtaining approval, adding an evidence label, or producing polished copy is not a new warrant.

Use four tests when a conclusion changes the strategic branch or evidence permission:

1. **Removal:** what survives if the preferred upstream premise is removed?
2. **Permission:** what did the source or prior decision actually permit us to infer?
3. **Rival:** what materially different situation or explanation remains viable?
4. **Fresh warrant:** why does this transition add support rather than recycle its own assumption?

State the results within the packet's reasoning; do not require another card schema for every descriptive fact. If a transition fails, narrow the conclusion, preserve branches, or reopen its origin. Do not fix a broken upstream model with downstream copy editing.

## Value, fit, and strategic forks

Ask whether the right customer could choose the complete value theme over a realistic alternative for that reason alone. This is a strategic counterfactual, not evidence that any customer actually did so. A feature can support the theme without passing that test individually.

Distinguish purchase-driving value from table stakes, qualification, proof requirements, and objection handling. A risk answer can be headline value when evidence shows a specific prior failure makes that risk central to the customer's choice; do not promote generic reassurance automatically.

Derive customer fit from the intensity of the value mechanism, then separately assess technical applicability, active buying opportunity, and proof readiness. A high-fit customer need not already have budget, a fully specified project, and an immediately testable deployment.

Different audiences may face different alternatives and derive different value from the same product. Keep one factual capability base, separate consequence/role hypotheses, and the actual purchase object. Different capabilities, product surfaces, purchase objects, or roadmaps may indicate a strategy fork. Test it; neither force one generic position nor invent separate products from different messaging alone.

## Challenge, supersession, and use

An evidence-based red team can test logic and comparative claims. A simulated buyer conversation is a diagnostic, not a real customer's reaction. A field-test plan is not a performed field test. Design live tests around falsifiers, actual alternatives, and the decisions customers make; do not conduct them without appropriate authorisation.

When evidence changes a premise, retain valid facts but invalidate affected conclusions and artifacts visibly. Mark historical outputs with their governing replacement. Keep a single identifiable current assembly; multiple legitimate branches need explicit identities and conditions.

Missing customer evidence cannot be repaired by stricter desk-research gates. Continue with a useful provisional model when authorised, while stating which judgments remain conditional. Completion of the exercise does not itself establish demand, adoption, conversion, achieved ROI, or company ownership.
